# Level 3 Cyber Security Practices: Security Controls and Data Protection

**Coursework dates:** 23 and 26 November 2026  
**Status:** Study notes and a simulated communication exercise. This is not a formal audit, legal assessment, or real security incident.

## CIS Controls and security culture

The lesson reviewed a 20-control grouping of the CIS Controls, organised into six basic, ten foundational, and four organisational controls. In this course material, foundational controls were presented as helping protect an organisation's assets. Organisational controls help establish and support a security culture through areas such as staff awareness and training, application security, incident response, and penetration testing.

Controls work best when they are applied as part of day-to-day practice. Staff need to understand their responsibilities, know how to report concerns, and follow agreed procedures. Controls should be reviewed and improved as risks and systems change.

## Data protection and cyber security

The GDPR and UK GDPR were studied in relation to protecting personal data. Security measures should be appropriate to the risks and to the information being handled. Examples discussed included:

- Limiting access to personal data to people who need it for their role
- Using encryption where appropriate
- Training staff to handle information securely
- Keeping reliable backups and checking that data can be restored
- Having a process to identify, report, and respond to data breaches

These are study notes about security practice, not legal advice or a claim that I assessed an organisation's compliance.

## Simulated security-remediation email

A short coursework email exercise considered four example issues:

1. An insecure application interface
2. A software backdoor
3. An overdue software update
4. An unlocked rear entrance

The response should explain the action needed in plain language: restrict and secure the interface, remove and investigate the backdoor, apply the update, and secure the entrance. It should assign an owner and deadline for each action, then verify that the fix has been completed.

The scenario is an exercise, not a real incident or assessment. It reinforced the value of concise reporting, clear ownership, prioritised actions, and follow-up checks.

## Reflection

Security controls support the protection of systems and information when people understand and use them consistently. Data protection depends on technical measures, staff awareness, physical security, and clear procedures for handling problems. A useful report turns identified issues into actions that have an owner, a deadline, and a way to confirm completion.
