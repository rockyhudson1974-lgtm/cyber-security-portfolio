# Cyber Security Foundations: Unit 1 Study Note

This note summarises learning from Unit 1, Section 1 of my NCFE Level 3 Certificate in Cyber Security Practices. It covers security objectives, identity, threats, vulnerabilities and risk.

## The CIA triad

The CIA triad is a simple way to describe three important aims of information security:

- **Confidentiality:** information is available only to people or systems that are authorised to access it.
- **Integrity:** information remains accurate, complete and protected from unauthorised changes.
- **Availability:** systems and information are accessible to authorised users when they are needed.

A security incident can affect one or more of these aims. For example, someone changing an order without permission affects integrity; an outage that prevents checkout affects availability.

## Identity and access

An identity represents a person, account or system. **Authentication** checks that identity, while **authorisation** determines what the authenticated identity is allowed to access or do. Keeping these ideas separate helps explain why proving who you are does not automatically mean you should have access to every resource.

## Threats, vulnerabilities and risk

- A **threat** is a potential cause of harm, such as an unauthorised person trying to access an account.
- A **vulnerability** is a weakness that could be exploited, such as an account with insufficient access controls.
- **Risk** considers the likelihood of a threat taking advantage of a vulnerability and the impact that could follow.
- A **hazard** is a source or condition with the potential to cause harm. In cyber security, accidental events and physical conditions can also create security risks.

### Hypothetical example

For an online shop, an exposed administrator account could be a vulnerability. An unauthorised login attempt is a threat. The risk depends on how likely the weakness is to be exploited and the impact—for example, changes to orders or exposure of customer information. This is an example for learning, not a finding about a real shop.

## What I learned

I learned to distinguish security objectives from the events and weaknesses that can put them at risk. Describing the asset, threat, vulnerability, likelihood and impact helps make a risk clearer and supports sensible decisions about protection.
